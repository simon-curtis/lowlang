std/big/decimal

#math

import std/big/decimal

A coefficient and an i32 exponent: exact add, sub and mul, and div and rescale with HalfEven, HalfUp, Down, Up, Floor or Ceiling rounding to a target exponent.

Imports std/big, std/text.

Types

Decimal

pub struct Decimal {
    ...
}

An exact decimal number coefficient * 10^exponent, with an arbitrary-precision coefficient and an i32 exponent.

The representation is not normalised: 1.0 and 1.00 have different exponents and compare equal. strip removes the trailing zeros of the coefficient.

Returns the exact sum of a and b.

cmp

pub fn cmp(a: Decimal, b: Decimal) -> i64

Compares the values of a and b and returns -1 when a < b, 0 when they are equal and 1 when a > b.

Exponents do not matter: 1.0 and 1.00 are equal.

rescale

pub fn rescale(d: Decimal, exponent: i32, mode: Rounding) -> Decimal

Returns d expressed with the given exponent.

When exponent is at most the exponent of d the result is exact. A larger exponent drops digits and rounds the result according to mode.

strip

pub fn strip(d: Decimal) -> Decimal

Returns d with the trailing zeros removed from its coefficient and added to its exponent.

Zero becomes exponent 0.

sub

The result has the smaller of the two exponents.

to_str

pub fn to_str(d: Decimal) -> str

Returns d in plain notation, never in scientific notation.

A negative exponent places the decimal point, so from_i64(5, -2) is "0.05", and a positive exponent writes out trailing zeros. A zero with a non-negative exponent is "0".

parse

pub fn parse(s: str) -> Decimal ! big.Error

Parses a decimal number.

The text is an optional + or -, digits with an optional . and fraction, and an optional e or E exponent with its own optional sign. Either the whole part or the fraction may be empty, but one of them needs a digit: .5 and 5. are valid. The exponent of the result is the parsed exponent minus the number of fractional digits, and trailing zeros are kept. Fails with Empty when there are no digits, with BadDigit carrying the byte offset of the first unexpected character, and with Overflow when the exponent does not fit an i32.
