std/time/date

#time

import std/time/date

Date with private fields and validated constructors (of, clamped, from_epoch_days, from_ordinal, from_iso_week, from_weekday_in_month). Month, Weekday and IsoWeek. plus and minus by Span with month-end clamping, until and since with a Diff, next_weekday and on_or_next, and range. ISO 8601 to_string and parse (calendar, ordinal and week dates). format and parse_with over a pattern.

Imports std/math, std/text, std/time/calendar, std/time/clock, std/time/duration, std/time/fault, std/time/pattern, std/time/rounding, std/time/scan, std/time/span, std/time/timestamp.

Date

pub struct Date {
    ...
}

A calendar date in the proleptic Gregorian calendar, from -9999-01-01 to 9999-12-31, with no time of day or zone.

Year 0 is 1 BC. A Date is always valid: the constructors reject a day that does not exist.

plus

pub fn plus(d: Date, s: span.Span) -> Date ! fault.Fault

Returns d moved forward by the span s.

Years and months are added first, with the day moved back to the last day of a shorter month, and then weeks and days. A negative span moves backwards. Fails with Invalid when s has hours, minutes, seconds or smaller units, and with OutOfRange when the result is outside the supported years.

until

pub fn until(a: Date, b: Date, diff: span.Diff) -> span.Span ! fault.Fault

Returns the span from a to b, measured and rounded as diff says.

The span is negative when b is earlier. Years and months are counted on the calendar from a, while weeks and days are exact. diff.largest must be a calendar unit and its default is Days. A smallest below days is treated as Days, and then only with an increment of 1. Fails with Invalid when the options are inconsistent and with OutOfRange or Overflow where the result cannot be represented.

until_with_time

pub fn until_with_time(a: Date, b: Date, extra: duration.Duration, diff: span.Diff) -> span.Span ! fault.Fault

Returns the span from the start of a to b plus the time of day extra, measured and rounded as diff says.

It serves the date-time types, which pass the difference of their times of day as extra. extra must be less than a day in magnitude and must not point the opposite way from the days between a and b. Years and months are counted on the calendar from a, while weeks, days and the time units are exact. Fails with Invalid when largest is not a calendar unit, when extra is out of bounds, or when the options are inconsistent as span.check_diff and span.check_time_increment state, and with OutOfRange or Overflow where rounding cannot represent the result.

IsoWeek

pub struct IsoWeek {The week-numbering year, which can differ from the calendar year near its ends.    pub year: i64The week of that year, from 1 to 53.    pub week: i64
}

An ISO 8601 week-numbering year and week.

Week 1 is the week with the year's first Thursday, so the first days of January can belong to the last week of the previous year and the last days of December to week 1 of the next.

from_fields

pub fn from_fields(f: pattern.Fields) -> Date ! fault.Fault

Returns the date in f.

The date comes from year, month and day, or else from year and day of year, or else from ISO year, ISO week and weekday. Other fields that are present, such as weekday or century, must agree with the date. Fails with Invalid when the fields do not name a date or conflict, and otherwise as of, from_ordinal and from_iso_week do.

parse

pub fn parse(s: str) -> Date ! fault.Fault

Parses a date in the ISO 8601 forms YYYY-MM-DD, YYYYMMDD, ordinal YYYY-DDD and YYYYDDD, and week dates YYYY-Www-D and YYYYWwwD.

The year has four digits and can start with -. Fails with Syntax at the offset of the first bad byte, with OutOfRange when a field is out of range and with Invalid when the day or week does not exist.
