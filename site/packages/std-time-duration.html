std/time/duration

#time

import std/time/duration

Duration, an exact signed elapsed time with private seconds and nanoseconds. Constructors from nanoseconds to hours, and from_seconds_f64. Arithmetic: plus, minus, times, divide, ratio, negate, abs, with try_ forms returning an optional. Plain forms panic on overflow. Also round and truncate with a Rounding, comparison, to_string (ISO 8601 PT1H30M), friendly (1h 30m) and parse, which reads both forms and fails at the offset of the first bad character.

Imports std/text, std/time/fault, std/time/rounding, std/time/scan.

Types

Duration

pub struct Duration {
    ...
}

An exact signed length of time with nanosecond precision.

It is held as whole seconds and a nanosecond part that always share a sign, with at most 999999999 nanoseconds. The seconds can be any i64 except the minimum. The plain arithmetic functions panic with duration: overflow when the result does not fit, and the try_ forms return none instead.

pub fn round(d: Duration, unit: Duration, mode: rounding.Rounding) -> Duration ! fault.Fault

Returns d rounded to a multiple of unit using mode.

Multiples are counted from zero. Fails with Invalid when unit is zero or negative, with OutOfRange when unit is longer than about 146 years (2^62 - 1 nanoseconds), and with Overflow when the rounded result does not fit.

pub fn to_string(d: Duration) -> str

Returns d in the ISO 8601 time form, such as PT1H30M or PT0.25S.

Zero is PT0S and a negative duration starts with -. Hours are not folded into days, and the seconds carry up to nine fraction digits with trailing zeros removed. parse reads the result back.

parse

pub fn parse(s: str) -> Duration ! fault.Fault

Parses a duration written in either the ISO 8601 form or the friendly form.

Both forms can start with + or -. The ISO form is PT followed by nH, nM and nS in that order, in either case, with a fraction allowed on the seconds only and at least one part present. The friendly form is one or more numbers each followed by a unit, ns, us, µs, ms, s, m or h, with optional spaces, such as 1h 30m or 1.5s. A fraction must be exactly representable in nanoseconds and have at most nine digits. Fails with Syntax at the offset of the first bad byte and with Overflow when the total does not fit.
