std/time/span

#time

import std/time/span

Span, a calendar amount of years, months, weeks, days and time units with one sign. Unit, Diff (largest, smallest, increment, mode), to_duration and from_duration, ISO 8601 to_string (P1Y2M3DT4H), friendly and parse.

Imports std/text, std/time/duration, std/time/fault, std/time/rounding.

Diff

pub struct Diff {The largest unit the result uses; an amount larger than it is counted in it, so Days gives 40 days and Weeks gives 5 weeks and 5 days. The default is Days.    pub largest: UnitThe smallest unit the result keeps; anything below it is rounded. It cannot be larger than largest. The default is Nanoseconds.    pub smallest: UnitThe result is rounded to a multiple of this many smallest units, from 1 to 1000000000. The default is 1.    pub increment: i64How the result is rounded to smallest and increment. The default is Trunc.    pub mode: rounding.Rounding
}

The options for measuring the difference between two dates or date-times as a Span.

A Diff with no fields set measures in days, keeps full precision and truncates. until and since on the date types check the options and fail with Invalid or OutOfRange when they are inconsistent.

Span

pub struct Span {
    ...
}

A length of time held as a separate count for each Unit, from years to nanoseconds.

The counts are kept as given and never carried into each other, so 90 minutes stays 90 minutes and 1 month stays a month until it is added to a date. All counts share one sign. Each count is limited to a bound: 19999 years, 239999 months, 1043999 weeks, 7304999 days, 175319999 hours, and larger bounds for the smaller units up to the range of i64 for nanoseconds. A Span can be compared with ==.

friendly

pub fn friendly(s: Span) -> str

Returns s in a short readable form, such as 1y 2mo 3d 4h 5m 6.5s.

Zero is 0s and a negative span starts with -. Only the nonzero counts are written, using y, mo, w, d, h, m and s, and the sub-second units are folded into fractional seconds. A span of only sub-second units is a single ms, us or ns count. parse reads the result back.

Unit

pub enum Unit {Calendar years.    YearsCalendar months.    MonthsWeeks of seven days.    WeeksCalendar days.    DaysHours of 3600 seconds.    HoursMinutes of 60 seconds.    MinutesSeconds.    SecondsThousandths of a second.    MillisecondsMillionths of a second.    MicrosecondsBillionths of a second.    Nanoseconds
}

A unit of time that a Span counts, from the largest, Years, to the smallest, Nanoseconds.

Years, Months, Weeks and Days are calendar units, whose length depends on the date they are applied to. The others are time units of a fixed length.

check_time_increment

pub fn check_time_increment(smallest: Unit, increment: i64, largest: Unit) ! fault.Fault

Checks that increment of smallest rounds evenly within the next larger unit.

For hours the increment must divide 24, for minutes and seconds 60, and for milliseconds, microseconds and nanoseconds 1000, and it must be smaller than that. Fails with Invalid otherwise. The check passes for calendar units and when largest is the same unit as smallest.

parse

pub fn parse(s: str) -> Span ! fault.Fault

Parses a span written in the ISO 8601 form or the friendly form.

Both forms can start with + or -. The ISO form is P followed by nY, nM, nW and nD, then an optional T and nH, nM and nS, in that order, in either case. The friendly form is one or more counts each followed by y, mo, w, d, h, m, s, ms, us, µs or ns, with optional spaces. Each unit appears at most once and in descending order. A fraction is allowed on a time unit only, ends the span, must be exact in nanoseconds and has at most nine digits. Fails with Syntax at the offset of the first bad byte and with OutOfRange or Invalid when a count is beyond its bound.

round_count

pub fn round_count(q: i64, class: i64, negative: bool, mode: rounding.Rounding) -> i64

Returns the count q rounded to q or q + 1 using mode, given how its fraction compares with one half as class from half_class.

q is a non-negative magnitude and negative says the value it stands for is below zero, so Floor and Ceil act on the signed value. A class of 0 returns q.
